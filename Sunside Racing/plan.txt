SUNSIDE RACING GAME PLAN

Status
- The asset pack, first playable world/driving slice, player saving,
  seeded, disk-cached world, world scenery dressing, distinct racing
  centers, and rudimentary traffic are built. Races are not built.
- This file preserves decisions; it is not permission to implement. A future
  game build still needs the user's direct "build" or "building" instruction.

Agreed world
- Top-down, open-world racing game on a 64 x 64 grid of scene-sized sectors.
- Load nearby sectors as the player drives. Keep distant sectors as lightweight
  map data; only nearby scenery, traffic, and collisions need to be active.
- Mainland: city center; jungle northwest; desert northeast; snow southwest;
  rural dirt region southeast. Beach borders the land, with sea extending to
  the world boundary. The player cannot leave the world or drive into the sea.
- A separate island sits offshore within the 64 x 64 grid and unlocks after
  all mainland races. The current world generator places it around sector
  (56, 31); its unlock and access mechanic remain open.
- The current prototype uses 512-pixel sectors, each containing an 8 x 8 grid
  of 64-pixel terrain tiles. Only camera-near sectors are generated; explored
  sectors are saved in world.json and loaded from disk on later launches.

Roads and scenery
- City: wide grid roads, buildings, and rudimentary traffic.
- Jungle and desert: no roads, fully off-road.
- Snow: winding icy roads. Rural: branching dirt roads. Beach: off-road.
- Road connections and scenery should line up across sector boundaries.

Racing progression
- One racing center in each mainland region: city, jungle, desert, snow,
  rural. Each center has 10 races with increasing difficulty (50 total).
- Completing all 50 mainland races unlocks the island's final five races.
- Races are distinct routes through the shared world, not separate maps.
- Race routes, opponents, rewards, and precise unlock rules beyond the island
  requirement remain to be designed.

Saving completed
- On normal exit, main.py saves the car's position and heading to player.json
  in the project root. On the next launch, it resumes there, stationary.
  Earlier saves in the operating system's user data directory can be loaded
  once and are written to the new project-root location on exit.
- player_save.py writes versioned JSON atomically. It validates finite
  coordinates, drivable terrain, and solid scenery on load; an absent or
  invalid save falls back to the starting position.
- world_save.py writes project-root world.json atomically. It stores the
  explicit world seed (default 2026) and generated sprites for only explored
  sectors. An existing world.json supplies the seed and those sectors on
  launch; unexplored sectors remain lazily generated from that seed.
- Traffic and other dynamic world state are not serialized yet.
- When car selection, currency, upgrades, and race results are built, extend
  the player save with those player-owned fields.

Assets completed
- Five PNG atlases and editable bitmap JSON sources are in assets/ and bitmap/:
  terrain, roads, vehicles, structures, and props (136 named sprites total).
- assets/atlas-manifest.json records atlas grids and shader tile coordinates.
- asset_sources.py is the deterministic source for the bitmap specifications.

First playable slice completed
- main.py runs a textured 64 x 64 world with camera follow and nearby sector
  generation. It displays the city, surrounding biomes, beach, sea, and island.
- The OpenGL window requests vsync, and vsync alone paces the game loop (see
  the tearing fix below).
- The player drives with WASD or arrows; Space applies a handbrake, R unsticks
  the car nearby, and Escape opens the pause menu. Grip and top speed vary by terrain. Sea, world bounds,
  buildings, and solid props block the car.
- This slice does not include traffic, race events, opponents, menus,
  or a way to reach the locked island. Those remain future work.

Tearing / frame pacing fix completed
- Diagnosed 2026-09-25 on an Apple M3 built-in 60 Hz display (pygame-ce
  2.5.7, SDL 2.32.10). Vsync works: flip() alone paces at 16.67 ms. The old
  extra clock.tick(60) cap fought vsync and dropped frames (33 ms spikes).
  Fractional camera positions made NEAREST-filtered tiles shimmer.
- main.py now uses clock.tick() (uncapped; vsync'd flip() paces frames, dt is
  still clamped to 0.05 s) and camera_position() rounds to whole pixels.
  TARGET_FPS was removed.
- Verified with a scripted 6-second drive: 16.67 ms mean, 0.29 ms stdev,
  18 ms worst frame, no frames over 20 ms; camera always on whole pixels.
- Remaining: a visual playtest. If tile seams still show, inset atlas UVs by
  half a texel in a game-local copy of the texture shader (shaders/ is shared
  and read-only). On a display faster than 60 Hz the game will run at that
  refresh rate; dt-based movement already handles this.

    rural_barn on a dirt_gravel yard, a 3 x 2 farm_rows field fenced with

  - Snow: snow_marker poles (non-solid) beside straight ice roads.
  - Ferry: beach_ferry_dock on the mainland beach at (47, 31) facing east and

    This is scenery only; the island access mechanic is still open.
  - Variety: jungle_tree_d, pine_d, cactus_c, rock_snow_b, hay_b, bush_dry,
    palm_c added to scatter; denser scatter and 64/72/80 px size variation.
- world_save.py records GENERATOR_VERSION (now 2). World saves from an older
  generator keep their seed but regenerate cached sectors lazily.
- Vehicle-atlas render capacity raised to 512 for parked cars.
- Still unused by design: race_* props, barrier, cone, city_start/finish,
  rural_start (race events); moving traffic and racers (traffic system);

Racing centers, speed, and traffic completed (2026-09-25)
- Racing centers: 160 px building (solid 104) on a 4 x 4 dark_asphalt plaza
  with a race_start gate on the south edge, race_finish gate on the north,
  solid barriers flanking open entries on every side, and corner cones.
  Scatter and city street poles are kept off the plaza. World generator
  version is now 3, so cached sectors regenerate.
- Guidance: hud.py draws a yellow screen-edge arrow (gl_utils polygon helpers
  with the shared line shaders) toward the current region's center while it
  is off screen; beaches point to the nearest center; no arrow at sea. The
  window caption shows the center name, distance (10 px = 1 m), and compass
  direction.
- Car top speeds lowered about 22% (city 340, island 310, rural 280,
  desert 265, snow 255, jungle 220, beach 210 px/s). Acceleration unchanged.
- Top speeds halved again for control (2026-09-25): city 170, island 155,
  rural 140, desert 132, snow 128, jungle 110, beach 105 px/s; reverse cap
  80 x grip. Acceleration and steering unchanged. Note: city traffic
  (140-180 px/s) is now about as fast as the player; open question whether
  to slow it too.
- Traffic (traffic.py), deterministic from the world seed (56 cars at
  first; see the busy city section for the current total):
  - City: 10 rectangular loops on the road grid, 3 cars each, 140-180 px/s.
  - Snow: 4 cars there-and-back on the ice road (100-120 px/s).
  - Rural: 4 cars on the main dirt road, 1 on each branch (120-140 px/s).
  - Jungle and desert: 3 off-road 4x4s each on wandering loops kept inside
    their region (85-110 px/s).
  - Extra pairs (added 2026-09-25, reusing existing sprites): 2 taxis,
    2 buses, 2 police cars, and 2 delivery vans, each pair on its own city
    loop, plus 2 SUVs on the snow road. They are generated after the
    original 46, so the original routes are unchanged.
  - Right-hand lanes with mitered corners and U-turns at dead ends.
  - Cars are solid to the player, stop short of the player, and queue
    behind a same-direction car ahead. Yielding runs only within 1600 px of
    the player.
- Known limits: cars crossing at intersections pass through each other, and
  off-road 4x4s drive through trees and rocks. Traffic is not saved.
- Tests: test_traffic.py covers lanes, road adherence, off-road regions, and
  yielding.

Nearby respawn and pause menu completed (2026-09-25)
- R now calls Car.respawn_nearby(): it searches outward in 8 px rings (up to
  480 px) for the closest spot where the car plus 16 px of clearance is
  drivable and free of scenery and traffic, keeps the heading, and stops the
  car. On a spot that is already clear it just stops the car. It returns to
  the start only if nothing nearby is free. Typical cost is 4-10 ms.
- pause_menu.py: Escape (or losing window focus) pauses the simulation and
  draws a dimmed world, a framed panel with a PAUSED title, and Resume / Exit
  buttons. W/S or arrows choose, Enter or Space confirms, Escape resumes, and
  mouse hover and click work via check_mouse_collisions. Exit saves and quits
  like closing the window. Drawn with the rect and tex gl_utils pipelines.
  Labels come from an in-memory text atlas made with pygame fonts and tinted
  per state.
- Tests: test_respawn_and_menu.py.

Parked-car commuters completed (2026-09-25)
- parking.py: in city lots within 2 sectors of the player, about 35% of
  nose-in parked cars (chosen stably per stall) are commuters. After 3-25 s
  (then 15-45 s between trips) one backs out of its stall to the aisle,
  drives to the nearest north-south road, laps one city block in the
  right-hand lane (away from the lot first so it returns straight), comes
  back, and pulls forward into the same stall.
- While a car is out, its stall is empty and not solid; the moving car is a
  TrafficCar (same sprite, 56 px, 18 x 38 solid). It is solid to the player
  and yields like other traffic. TrafficCar gained one-way trips,
  reverse-driven segments, and per-car size and solid box.
- Trips already underway finish even if the player drives off; parked timers
  reset for lots the player has left. Nothing new is saved: static parked
  sprites stay in world.json, so every car is parked on relaunch.
- Tests: test_parking.py (including every commuter route avoiding buildings
  and centers). Parking and traffic updates cost about 0.26 ms per frame.
- Known limits: nose-out parked cars (180 degrees) never move, and
  commuters pass through other cars in the lot aisle.

Busy city traffic completed (2026-09-25)
- Target mix agreed: busy city, quiet rural and snow, very quiet jungle and
  desert. 70 extra city loops (EXTRA_CITY_LOOPS, 3 cars each) are generated
  after all earlier traffic, so earlier routes are unchanged. Rural (6),
  snow (6), jungle (3), and desert (3) are unchanged. Total 266 cars: 248 in
  the city. Plus parked commuters.
- Measured: about 9 cars on screen in the city on average (max about 18).
  Traffic and parking updates cost about 0.9 ms per frame.
- Intersection yielding: a car about to enter a city intersection box
  (60 x 60 px) waits if a perpendicular car is inside it or claimed it
  first. Cars already inside never stop for the crossing.
- Queues behind the player or a car ahead always hold, so cars never drive
  through each other in a queue, and a player stopped in a lane backs up
  traffic. Only intersection waits give up after 4 s (PATIENCE), which
  prevents gridlock; this happened about 5 times per simulated minute. A
  stress run (8 simulated minutes, 4 busy spots) had no car stall 10 s.
- Tests: test_traffic.py adds the regional mix, intersection yielding, and a
  no-gridlock check.

Planned: on-foot mode with pedestrians and NPCs (agreed 2026-09-25, not built)
- Idea: the player can exit the car and walk around. Pedestrians walk fixed
  paths (sidewalks, crosswalks, into and out of buildings). Select NPCs can
  be interacted with.
- Performance: fine at this scale. A frame is about 2.6 ms render plus
  0.9 ms traffic out of 16.7 ms. Packing costs about 1.4 us per on-screen
  sprite, so even 1,000 on-screen sprites add only about 1.4 ms.
  Pedestrians should be simulated only near the player (like parked
  commuters) with no all-pairs avoidance checks.
- Likely pieces:
  - New pedestrian/NPC sprite atlas (PNG skill), drawn above props and
    below vehicles.
  - Sidewalk and crosswalk walk paths; door points on buildings (a
    pedestrian "enters" by reaching a door and despawning, later
    reappearing).
  - On-foot player: small collision box, walking speed, E to exit or enter
    the car. The parked player car becomes a world obstacle.
  - NPC interaction: proximity prompt plus a dialogue panel reusing the
    pause-menu UI style.
  - Cheap wins first: throttle HUD text re-renders (about 1 ms to 0.1 ms)
    and tighten the sprite gather margin.
- Decisions (2026-09-25):
  - Camera zooms in while on foot (proposed 2x; exact level still open).
  - Buildings: doors only. Pedestrians walk to a door, disappear, and later
    reappear. Interiors only for a few buildings the user will choose later.
  - NPCs: mostly tiny missions (short fetch, deliver, or drive-to tasks),
    started by walking up to an NPC and pressing E.
  - Cars stop for pedestrians (same yield rule traffic uses for the
    player); pedestrians cannot be hit.
- Build order (steps 1 and 2 done 2026-09-25):
  1. DONE: render wins. world.visible_sprites gathers only sectors touching
     the view plus a 96 px margin (GATHER_MARGIN): 1,109 sprites gathered
     instead of 2,588, 0.37 ms to 0.17 ms. HUD text re-renders at most every
     0.2 s (speed every 0.1 s). The real HUD cost was reusing one instance
     buffer for every label in a frame (each write waited for the GPU); each
     label now has its own buffer: HUD 1.09 ms to 0.09 ms. The whole frame
     went from 2.63 ms to 1.67 ms (vsync off, city start).
  2. DONE: on foot.
     - E beside a nearly stopped car (under 15 px/s) steps out on the
       driver's side (else passenger, back, front); E within 44 px of the car
       gets back in.
     - walker.py: W/A/S/D move in screen directions at 60 px/s (Shift runs
       at 105), slide along walls, and use a 12 px collision box and
       2-frame walk animation.
     - While on foot the parked car is solid (CollisionManager.fixed) and
       traffic stops for it and for the walker (Traffic.update blockers).
     - The camera eases to 2x zoom on foot and back to 1x in the car.
       GameState.render scales positions and sizes by zoom, and the camera
       snaps to whole screen pixels.
     - HUD hides the speedometer on foot and shows a bottom-center prompt
       ("E  Get in"), reusable for NPC prompts. R unsticks the walker too
       (shared nearest_clear_spot).
     - New people-atlas.png (32 px cells: player_idle, player_walk_a,
       player_walk_b) from asset_sources.people(); a red jacket and cream cap
       with a dark red brim. Drawn above props and below vehicles.
     - The Controls help page lists Shift and E and the on-foot directions.
     - Known: a car parked in a traffic lane backs up traffic until you
       move it.
  3. Pedestrian sprite atlas and sidewalk/crosswalk/door walking with
     spawning near the player; cars yield to them.
  4. NPC prompt, dialogue panel, and a small mission system.
  5. Interiors for buildings the user picks.
- Still open: exact zoom level; which buildings get interiors; the first

Technical basis
- Keep game-specific code in this marked project. Adapt game_state.py,
  input_handler.py, and collision_manager.py and compose them from main.py.
- Use the documented gl_utils textured-rectangle records, load_texture,
  get_new_instances, to_gl/update_instances, and check_collision where needed.
- Use the atlas manifest's bottom-origin tile coordinates with the shared
  texture shader and its u_atlas_grid uniform.
- main.py composes world.py, car.py, game_state.py, input_handler.py, and
  collision_manager.py; player_save.py and world_save.py handle persistence. The active renderer
  batches instances by atlas and streams camera-relative records through the
  documented GL helpers.
